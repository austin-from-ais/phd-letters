For Hyo Kang, from Austin

Notes for your letter

You have been my closest collaborator since I was a master's student, and most of what I know about research I learned from you. This page puts the record in one place and says which questions a committee is most likely to bring to your letter.

The letter is yours and should say what you think. Nothing here is wording to use.

What your letter covers that the others cannot

Nigel Newbutt is writing about the work with autistic adults and the project I led with the Massachusetts networks. Tim DiFato is writing about my teaching and what I have contributed to the Digital Worlds Institute. You have seen the research from the inside, from the start.

  • That we designed the work together

    I am second or third author on most of our papers, and a committee cannot tell from an author list that the projects and the research programs around them were designed jointly. You can say which ideas, designs and decisions were mine.

  • That I can run a lab

    You have watched me lead students, organize several teams on XR projects, keep regular updates going, and manage relationships between lab groups and stakeholders across campus. For a committee asking whether I can carry a doctoral project, that is direct evidence.

  • How far I have come

    You taught me and have watched the whole development, from a first-year master's student to now. No one else can describe that arc or say what it suggests about the next five years.

  • The direction I want to take in a PhD

    My statement proposes building a model of the learner from headset data, with a language model reasoning over it. The tutoring system and eye-tracking work we are doing now is the start of that. You can say the direction is real and already under way.

Where your judgment counts most

  • A comparison with the doctoral students you have supervised. Stanford's form asks for this directly, as a rating against a group you name.
  • One or two specific contributions: an idea, a design decision or a piece of analysis that was mine and changed how a project or a paper turned out.
  • A moment when you saw me lead: a team I organized, a problem between groups that I sorted out, a student I brought along.
  • Why a PhD now, given that I already work in your group. What doctoral training would give me that our collaboration cannot.
  • Stanford's prompt is a fair checklist for every school: potential for advanced study, intellectual independence, analytical thinking, clear expression, and teaching potential.
